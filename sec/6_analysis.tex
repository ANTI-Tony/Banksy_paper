\subsection{Where \method fails}
\label{sec:failures}
\paragraph{Breaking correct text.} \method breaks 15 of the 284 originally correct clips (\steer: 6). In all 15, the evaluation reader transcribes the original perfectly while the pipeline's OCR reads it correctly in almost no frame (neon italics, extruded or condensed letters), so the preservation gate cannot fire. Inspecting the 15 outputs (\cref{fig:fail}, last three rows) gives five failure types: two-row text whose second row overwrote part of the first during compositing (3 clips; fixed in the code, \pending{re-run pending}); new text larger than its sign, so that letters fall off the carrier (3); wrong colour or contrast on lightboxes and extruded letters (2); a second row that was not detected, leaving the old text visible next to the new one (4); and a neon glyph rendered as a blob (1). The remaining 2 clips are borderline cases in which the reader additionally transcribes small neighbouring text in 2 of the 5 frames.

\paragraph{Readable but not faithful.} The more common failure does not affect readability (\cref{fig:fail}, first three rows): erasure leaves a visible patch, or the new glyphs are flat where the old ones were outlined, extruded or glowing. These cases are frequent in the random sample of \cref{fig:qual} and explain the appearance distance in \cref{tab:main}.

\subsection{The appearance gap to generative editing}
\label{sec:limits}
Among the 148 garbled clips that both methods repair, \steer's text is closer to the original appearance in 71\% (median appearance distance 0.89 vs.\ 1.77), so the gap is not an artefact of \steer leaving garbled text untouched. We see four causes, in decreasing order of visibility.
\begin{itemize}
\item \textbf{Rendering instead of generation.} \method can reproduce colour, shading and, with SAT, glow; it cannot reproduce extrusion, bevels, tube geometry or texture inside the strokes, which a generator synthesises naturally.
\item \textbf{A small font library.} The library holds 33 Latin and 8 Chinese families, and the generator's typefaces are invented. In practice, 26 Latin families are selected, but for Chinese only three, and Noto Sans SC alone renders 68\% of the Chinese clips; the result reads correctly but looks uniform.
\item \textbf{Axis-aligned layout.} The letter band inherits the orientation of the OCR quad, which is axis-aligned for most detectors, so on tilted carriers the new text can look skewed relative to the sign (\cref{fig:teaser}a); the perspective estimate of an earlier front end was too fragile to keep (supplement).
\item \textbf{Erasure traces.} LaMa fills the hole once per clip; on textured or shaded carriers the fill is visible as a patch.
\end{itemize}
Two observations suggest that the gap is closable without giving up correctness. First, the garbled source already contains most of the target's characters: in garbled English clips, a median of 88\% of the target letters occur in the reader's transcription of the original (Chinese: 67\%). Reusing these glyphs---cut from the source with their own font, colour, lighting and perspective---and synthesising only the missing ones would extend the ``change only what is wrong'' principle from words to characters. Second, the two methods fail on different clips: the share of clips that at least one of them repairs is 97.1\%. A combination that accepts a generative edit only when independent OCR verifies it, and otherwise falls back to deterministic repair, is a natural next step; its verifier must be strict, since accepting a wrong edit costs correctness, whereas rejecting a right one costs only appearance.

\subsection{Other limitations}
The benchmark is generated by one family of models and its prompts ask for short sign texts; a second generator (CogVideoX-5B, 360 clips from the same prompts) is part of our plan but has not been evaluated yet, and long texts such as menus or user interfaces are out of scope. Readability is measured by one VLM reader; it favours large, high-contrast text, which may flatter the box policy, and a second reader is needed. The human study on SAT used four raters after quality control, and the study comparing \method with \steer on naturalness and on damage to non-target content is still being collected. Finally, the preservation gate is safe but has low recall, because it relies on a conventional OCR that rarely reads stylised originals.
