\subsection{External benchmarks}
\label{sec:exp_external}
\input{tables/tab_external}
We re-ran both external benchmarks with the frozen final system (B1, the configuration of \cref{tab:main}, run from a code snapshot) and keep the earlier runs with a superseded front end only as history (supplement). All methods of a benchmark are evaluated in one evaluation run (\cref{tab:external}). Neither benchmark matches our task exactly, so we state what each method receives.

\paragraph{Editing correct text (VTE-Bench Real).} The 100 real clips of \steer's own benchmark~\cite{zeng2026steervte} ask to change correct text into a different string. \method receives the source clip, the target string, the source string (used only to locate the text by OCR) and the benchmark's text mask as hint box; \steer receives the mask, a rendering of the target and an image of the source style, following its official protocol. In \xVteFails{} of the 100 clips the frozen front end does not find enough detection boxes inside the hint and ships the clip unchanged, as it would in deployment; these clips are scored as unchanged and listed in the supplement. On this task \method is clearly behind \steer: \xVteOurs\% against \xVteSteer\% readability under the first reader (\xVteOnlyOurs{} vs.\ \xVteOnlySteer{} discordant clips, $p=\xVteP$), \xVteOursIvl\% against \xVteSteerIvl\% under the second ($p=\xVtePIvl$), and \xVteOursOcr\% against \xVteSteerOcr\% under EasyOCR. It changes less outside the text (\xVteOursBG{} vs.\ \xVteSteerBG\,dB) and flickers and jitters less, but the unlocated clips alone cost up to \xVteFails{} points, and on correct, well-formed source text the gates that make \method safe for repair have nothing to protect, whereas \steer is trained for exactly this replacement. We report the result to delimit our claim: \method is a repair method for garbled generated text, not a general video text editor.

\paragraph{Commercial video models (VidScribe).} VidScribe~\cite{vidscribe2026} releases the outputs of seven commercial video models for video-to-video text editing. We use the 44 of its 93 clips whose operation is a replacement, translation or deletion and whose source text can be located by OCR (27 style-only and 9 insertion clips are excluded by operation, 13 by localisation); \method receives the source clip, the target string, the source string and the located box, whereas the commercial models were prompted with an instruction and receive no box. The frozen front end does not locate \xVsFails{} clip, which is shipped unchanged. Deletions have an empty target, so readability is undefined for them: we report readability on the 29 replacement and translation clips and, for the 15 deletions, the share in which the source string is no longer read (similarity below 0.5 in at least 4 of 5 frames). On the 29 clips, \method reads correctly in \xVsOurs\% under the first reader (\xVsOursIvl\% under the second), ahead of the strongest commercial model (\xVsBestName, \xVsBest\%) without significance (\xVsBestOnlyOurs{} vs.\ \xVsBestOnlyThem{} discordant clips, $p=\xVsBestP$) and significantly ahead of \xVsSig{} of the seven models ($p<0.05$). It removes the source text in \xVsOursRem\% of the deletions. The commercial models regenerate the whole clip and lower the PSNR outside the text region to \xVsBGLo--\xVsBGHi\,dB, against \xVsOursBG\,dB for \method, and they flicker more; their text is, however, closer in appearance to the original (lowest appearance distance: wan-3.0). Absolute readability is low for every method because most VidScribe targets are long phrases.
